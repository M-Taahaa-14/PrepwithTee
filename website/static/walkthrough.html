The complete walkthrough

Everything on this site,
in the order you should use it.

PrepWithTee has a lot of tools — a topical paper builder, a full past-paper archive, chapter notes, a progress tracker, an AI tutor and mock tests. This page walks you through which one to open, when, and what to actually do inside it. Follow it top to bottom and you'll never wonder what to study next.

Step 01 5 minutes · once

Set up your account and pick your board

Everything on PrepWithTee is filtered by the qualification you're actually sitting. Tell us once — O Level, IGCSE or A Level — and your dashboard, progress tracker and paper builder stop showing you syllabuses you'll never sit.

  • Create your account and fill in the short profile — name and qualification are all that's required.
  • Add your subjects on the dashboard. Only your board's subjects appear, so you can't add the wrong syllabus by accident.
  • Sitting a different board? Change it in your profile and the whole site re-filters instantly — nothing you've done is lost.
Go here Dashboard Edit profile
💡
Tee's tipAdd every subject you're sitting now, even the ones you feel fine about. The dashboard progress rings are only useful when they show the whole picture.
Step 02 20 minutes · once per subject

Read the syllabus before you study a single page

Cambridge tells you exactly what it will examine. Most students never read it, then waste weeks on topics that were removed years ago. The official syllabus PDF for every subject is in Resources.

  • Download your syllabus and skim the subject content list — that list is your chapter list.
  • Check the paper structure: how many papers, how long, how many marks, which are calculator-allowed.
  • Note what changed. Recent syllabuses drop topics — for O Level Maths, Loci and Matrices are gone; Surds and Exponential growth are in. Don't practise dead topics.
Go here Resources → Official Syllabuses Subjects overview
Step 03 30 minutes · your baseline

Map every chapter — honestly — in Progress Tracker

Progress Tracker lists every chapter of your syllabus with a Not started / Learning / Confident switch. Go through the whole list in one sitting and mark where you genuinely stand. This is the single most valuable half-hour of your prep, because from here on the site knows what to point you at.

  • Be brutal. "I've seen it in class" is Not started. Confident means you'd get full marks under time pressure.
  • Mark subtopics too where they're listed — that's how you find the one weak area inside an otherwise fine chapter.
  • Watch your dashboard rings fill as you convert chapters to Confident. That percentage is your real revision progress.
Go here Progress Tracker Dashboard rings
🎯
Why this mattersStudents who study "everything" run out of time and revise their strong chapters most, because those are the comfortable ones. The tracker forces you to spend your hours where the marks are.
Step 04 Per chapter · learning phase

Learn the theory, chapter by chapter

Take one chapter marked Not started or Learning — start with a weak one, not a favourite. Read the notes, watch the linked revision video if there is one, and write your own one-page summary. Don't try to memorise; aim to be able to explain it out loud.

  • Open Resources and filter to your subject for chapter notes, formula sheets and revision videos.
  • Make one page of your own notes per chapter — definitions, formulae, and the two or three things you always get wrong.
  • Keep it beside you while you do Step 5. Looking things up while practising is learning; guessing is not.

Step 05 The core habit · every study session

Build a topical past-paper booklet and solve it

This is the heart of PrepWithTee. Instead of solving whole papers where one chapter appears once, the builder pulls every real Cambridge question ever asked on your chosen chapters and composes them into one PDF booklet — original diagrams, graphs and answer lines untouched, with the mark scheme attached.

  • Pick your subject, tick the one or two chapters you just learned.
  • Set the year range. Recent years first (2022–2025) — they match the current syllabus style most closely.
  • Choose the paper (P1 vs P2, or a component) so you practise the format you'll actually sit.
  • Download and print it. Write on paper, with a pen, the way the exam works. Screen practice hides how slow your working really is.
Go here Topical Paper Builder
📄
Do this in orderOne chapter at a time, oldest-to-newest questions. You'll physically see how the examiner's phrasing repeats — that pattern recognition is what separates a B from an A*.
Step 06 After every booklet · non-negotiable

Mark yourself against the real mark scheme

Every booklet ships with the official mark scheme. Marking is where the learning happens — not in the solving. Give yourself marks the way an examiner would: method marks for working, accuracy marks for the answer, nothing for "I basically knew it".

  • Mark in a different colour so mistakes stay visible when you come back to the booklet.
  • For every lost mark, write why in the margin: didn't know it, misread it, or careless. Three different problems, three different fixes.
  • Re-mark the chapter in Progress Tracker. Full marks → Confident. Anything below → stay on Learning and come back to it.

Step 07 Same day · never leave it

Clear every doubt the same day you find it

A question you couldn't do is a gap, and gaps compound — the next chapter builds on this one. The AI Tutor answers concept questions and works through problems step by step, and the photo solver reads a question straight off your paper.

  • Ask the AI Tutor to explain the concept, not just the answer — "why does this step work?" beats "what's the answer?".
  • Stuck on a printed question? Photograph it in the solver and get examiner-style working, mark by mark.
  • Get quizzed. Progress Tracker's Quiz me generates an exam-style question on any chapter and marks your answer against the real criteria.
  • Still stuck after all that? That's what a human tutor is for — message Tee.
Go here AI Tutor Photo solver Quiz me Ask Tee
Step 08 Weekly · once a chapter is Confident

Test yourself with a timed mock — answers hidden

A topical booklet has the mark scheme right there, which is perfect for learning and useless for testing. A generated mock test picks a random set of questions to a mark target and puts the mark scheme in a separate file, so you can genuinely sit it.

  • Set a mark target, not a question count — the suggested time is roughly a minute a mark, exactly like the real exam.
  • Sit it in one go, timed, no notes. Phone in another room.
  • Only then open the mark scheme. Score it, and log what you lost marks on.
  • Repeat the same chapters a fortnight later — a different random pick each time proves whether it stuck.
Go here Paper builder → Mock test mode
⏱️
Timing is a skillMost students lose more marks to running out of time than to not knowing the content. You can only train that under a real clock.
Step 09 Final 6–8 weeks

Switch to full past papers, under exam conditions

Once most of your chapters are Confident, stop doing topicals and start doing whole papers. The library has every original question paper and mark scheme, by year and session. This is where you learn to switch between topics mid-paper — the thing topical practice can't teach you.

  • One full paper per subject per week, strictly timed, in one sitting.
  • Mark it the same day and record the score. You want the trend, not one number.
  • Turn every mistake back into a topical. Lost marks on circle theorems? Build a circle-theorem booklet the next day.
  • Save the two most recent years for the fortnight before the exam — those are your truest predictor.

What a good study week actually looks like

Four sessions a week per subject beats one long panic session. Here's the shape of it.

Session 1 · Learn

One new chapter. Notes, video, and your own one-page summary. No questions yet.

Session 2 · Practise

Build a topical booklet on that chapter, 2022–2025. Solve it with your notes open.

Session 3 · Mark & fix

Mark against the scheme, write down why each mark was lost, clear the doubts with the tutor.

Session 4 · Test

Timed mock test covering this chapter plus two older ones. Mark scheme stays shut until you're done.

Counting down to the exam

The same tools, used differently depending on how much time is left.

6+ months out

Build the base

  • Map all chapters in Progress Tracker
  • Learn + topical-practise one chapter at a time
  • Widest year range — depth over speed
  • Don't touch full papers yet

2–6 months out

Close the gaps

  • Target every chapter still on Learning
  • Weekly timed mock tests by mark target
  • Mixed-chapter booklets, not single chapters
  • First full paper, untimed, to see the shape

Final 6–8 weeks

Exam conditions

  • One full timed paper per subject per week
  • Every mistake becomes a same-week topical
  • Save the two newest years for the last fortnight
  • Re-read your own one-page summaries daily

Questions students actually ask

The ones that come up in almost every first lesson.

I only see some subjects — where are the rest?
The dashboard and progress tracker show only the subjects for the qualification on your profile — O Level, IGCSE or A Level. That's deliberate: it stops you building a booklet from the wrong syllabus. If you're sitting a different board, or a mix, open your profile, change the qualification and save. The whole site re-filters straight away, and nothing you've already tracked is lost.
Topical booklets or full past papers — which should I do?
Topical until most of your chapters are Confident, then full papers. Doing whole papers early feels productive but teaches you very little: you meet each chapter once, in a random order, and half the paper is on things you haven't learned yet. Topical practice is what builds the pattern recognition; full papers train stamina and timing at the end.
How many years should I include in a booklet?
Start with the most recent three or four years — they match the current syllabus and the current examiner style. Once you've cleared those and want more volume, widen the range. If your syllabus changed recently, be careful with older papers: some questions cover topics that have since been removed.
Do I have to print the booklets?
You don't have to, but you should. The exam is handwritten under time pressure, and solving on a screen quietly hides how slow your working is and how much you rely on scrolling back. Print it, use a pen, mark in a second colour.
What's the difference between a topical booklet and a mock test?
A topical booklet gives you every question on your chosen chapters with the mark scheme right there — it's for learning. A mock test takes a random subset to a mark target and puts the mark scheme in a separate file — it's for testing. Use the first while you're building the skill, the second to prove you have it.
How do I know if I'm actually improving?
Two signals. Your dashboard rings show what share of each syllabus you've moved to Confident — that's coverage. Your mock test scores over time show whether it's holding up under pressure — that's retention. You want both climbing; if coverage climbs and scores don't, you're marking yourself too generously.

That's the whole system.

Nine steps, five tools, one habit: learn a chapter, practise it on real questions, mark it honestly, fix what broke. Do that on repeat and the grade takes care of itself.
