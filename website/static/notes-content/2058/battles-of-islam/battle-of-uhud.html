Overview

Battle of Uhud — the second major battle; a severe trial for the Muslim community that exposed the consequences of disobeying the Prophet ﷺ.
Date7 Shawwal, 3 AH (March 625 CE)
LocationThe slopes of Mount Uhud, north of Madinah
Muslim forceInitially 1,000; reduced to 700 when Abdullah ibn Ubayy withdrew with 300 hypocrites
Quraysh force~3,000 men, led by Abu Sufyan ibn Harb
OutcomeMuslims suffered heavy losses — a partial defeat

Background

The Quraysh sought revenge for their humiliating defeat at Badr. Abu Sufyan organised a larger, better-equipped army and marched on Madinah. Some Companions wanted to defend from within Madinah; others, keen to fight in the open, urged the Prophet ﷺ to meet the enemy in the field. He chose to march out to Uhud.

Key events

  1. Archers stationed: The Prophet ﷺ placed 50 archers under Abdullah ibn Jubayr (RA) on a small hill (known as Ainain Hill or Jabal al-Rumah). His explicit order: "Do not leave this position whether you see us winning or losing."
  2. Early Muslim success: The initial charge was fierce; the Quraysh began to retreat and some fled. The Muslim cavalry of Khalid ibn Walid (not yet Muslim, fighting for Quraysh) was held back by the archers.
  3. Archers disobey: Seeing apparent victory, most archers abandoned their position to collect spoils of war, leaving the rear exposed. Only Abdullah ibn Jubayr and a handful remained.
  4. Khalid ibn Walid's counter-attack: He led his cavalry through the now unguarded pass, attacking the Muslims from behind. The Quraysh rallied. Muslims were caught between two forces.
  5. The Prophet ﷺ was injured: His front teeth were broken; his face was cut; he fell into a pit. A rumour spread that he had been killed — causing some to flee or stop fighting.
  6. Hamza (RA) martyred: The Prophet's uncle and the "Lion of Allah" was killed by Wahshi ibn Harb. His body was mutilated by Hind bint Utbah (Abu Sufyan's wife), who had offered Wahshi a reward.
  7. Muslim rally: When the Companions confirmed the Prophet ﷺ was alive, they rallied around him. The battle ended without a full Qurayshi victory — they withdrew towards Makkah rather than advancing into Madinah.

Outcome

Casualties:
  • Muslims: 70 martyred, including Hamza ibn Abd al-Muttalib (RA)
  • Quraysh: 22–23 killed

Lessons from Uhud

  • Obedience to the Prophet ﷺ is essential — the archers' disobedience was the direct cause of the reversal. This is cited in Surah Al-Imran 3:152: "And Allah had certainly fulfilled His promise to you when you were killing the enemy by His permission, until [the time] when you lost courage and fell to disputing about the order…"
  • Greed for worldly gain (dunya) is dangerous — the archers left for spoils, prioritising the world over obedience to Allah's command.
  • A trial, not a punishment — Uhud was a test from Allah. Surah Al-Imran 3:139–140 reassures believers: "So do not weaken and do not grieve, and you will be superior if you are [true] believers."
  • Hypocrites (munafiqun) exposed — Abdullah ibn Ubayy's withdrawal revealed who truly believed and who did not.
  • The Prophet ﷺ's conduct — even whilst injured, he prayed for his enemies: "O Allah, forgive my people, for they do not know."
Exam questions often ask: "Why did the Muslims face difficulties at Uhud?" The key point is the archers abandoning their post — but a full answer also mentions Abdullah ibn Ubayy's withdrawal, the larger Quraysh force, and the rumour of the Prophet's death causing panic. Give all four reasons and rank them.
Uhud was not a total defeat — the Quraysh did not capture Madinah, and the Muslim community survived. Avoid writing that the Muslims "lost" without qualification. Examiners look for the nuance that it was a setback caused by human error, not a failure of Islam.
Model paragraph: significance of the archers' disobedience

The most significant cause of Muslim difficulties at Uhud was the disobedience of the archers. The Prophet ﷺ had stationed fifty archers on Ainain Hill with a clear command: "Do not leave your position whether you see us winning or losing." When most of the archers abandoned their post to collect spoils of war, Khalid ibn Walid led his cavalry through the unguarded pass and attacked the Muslims from the rear. This single act of disobedience turned a near-victory into a severe loss of seventy martyrs. The Quran directly referenced this in Surah Al-Imran (3:152), teaching Muslims that following the Prophet's ﷺ commands — even when victory appears certain — is an absolute obligation. The lesson of Uhud thus reinforced the supremacy of obedience over personal judgement.
