Overview

Conquest of Makkah (Fath al-Makkah) — the peaceful capture of Makkah by the Muslim army, fulfilling the Prophet's ﷺ return to his birthplace and completing the purification of the Kaaba.
Date20 Ramadan, 8 AH (January 630 CE)
Muslim army~10,000 — the largest Muslim force to date
Makkah's defendersMinimal resistance; Quraysh forces largely dispersed
OutcomePeaceful capture of Makkah; general amnesty declared

Background and causes

Treaty of Hudaybiyyah (6 AH / 628 CE): The Prophet ﷺ and Abu Sufyan had signed a 10-year peace treaty at Hudaybiyyah. The key clause: both sides and their allies would not attack each other.

Cause for the Conquest: The Quraysh broke the treaty in late 8 AH by assisting the Banu Bakr tribe in an unprovoked attack on the Banu Khuza'a — who were allies of the Muslims. This was a clear violation of the treaty, giving the Prophet ﷺ legal and moral grounds to march on Makkah.

Preparation and march

  1. The Prophet ﷺ maintained strict secrecy about the destination of the campaign — even within Madinah — to achieve surprise.
  2. Abu Sufyan ibn Harb, the Quraysh leader, came to Madinah to attempt renewed negotiations. He met the Prophet ﷺ and, witnessing the scale and faith of the Muslim community, accepted Islam.
  3. The Prophet ﷺ granted Abu Sufyan a gesture of security: anyone who stays inside their house, inside the Mosque, or at Abu Sufyan's house would be safe — a strategic move that prevented widespread resistance.
  4. 10,000 Muslims marched in four columns, approaching Makkah simultaneously from different directions. The sight of the army camped around Makkah stunned the Quraysh into submission.

The Day of Conquest

  1. The Prophet ﷺ entered Makkah with his head bowed in humility and gratitude, reciting Surah Al-Fath. He performed tawaf (circling the Kaaba).
  2. The only significant fighting occurred at one gate, where Khalid ibn Walid (by then a Muslim commander) faced brief resistance; 12 Quraysh fighters were killed.
  3. The Prophet ﷺ stood at the door of the Kaaba and addressed the Quraysh who had gathered, asking: "O people of Quraysh, what do you think I shall do to you?" They replied: "You are a noble brother and a noble nephew." He declared: "Go — for you are all free." (In Arabic: Idhhabu fa antum al-tulaqaa')
  4. 360 idols that surrounded the Kaaba were destroyed. As each was struck, the Prophet ﷺ recited: "Truth has come and falsehood has vanished. Indeed, falsehood is ever bound to vanish." (Surah Al-Isra 17:81)
  5. Bilal ibn Rabah (RA) — the freed former slave — gave the first adhan (call to prayer) from atop the Kaaba. This was deeply symbolic: the man who had been tortured for accepting Islam was now calling the faithful to prayer from the most sacred structure on earth.

Significance

  • General amnesty — the Prophet ﷺ forgave those who had persecuted, tortured and killed Muslims for 20 years. This unprecedented act of mercy is considered one of the finest examples of his character and the Islamic principle of 'afw (forgiveness).
  • Purification of the Kaaba — the House of Allah was cleared of idols and restored to the pure monotheism of Ibrahim (AS). This fulfilled a centuries-old aspiration.
  • Mass conversion — the Quraysh and virtually the entire population of Makkah accepted Islam. The conquest triggered a wave of conversions across Arabia (Surah An-Nasr 110:1–2: "When the victory of Allah has come and the conquest, and you see the people entering into the religion of Allah in multitudes…").
  • Islamic equality demonstrated — Bilal's adhan showed that race and social class had no standing in Islam.
  • Completion of the Hijaz — Makkah, Madinah and Taif now under Muslim authority; the Arabian Peninsula would be unified under Islam within two years.
Surah Al-Fath (48:1)
"Indeed, We have given you a clear conquest."
Revealed after Hudaybiyyah but applicable to the Conquest. Used in essays about the significance of Fath al-Makkah.
For an "Evaluate the significance of the Conquest of Makkah" essay, structure around: (1) scale — peaceful, 10,000-strong army; (2) general amnesty — example of Islamic mercy; (3) purification of the Kaaba; (4) mass conversions; (5) unification of Arabia. Your conclusion should judge which was most significant and why.
Do not describe the Conquest as a "war" or "battle" — it was largely peaceful. The examiner expects the word "conquest" and expects you to highlight the absence of large-scale violence as one of its most remarkable features.
