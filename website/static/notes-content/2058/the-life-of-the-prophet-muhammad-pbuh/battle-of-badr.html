Overview

Battle of Badr — the first major military engagement between the Muslims of Madinah and the Quraysh of Makkah.
Date17 Ramadan, 2 AH (March 624 CE)
LocationThe wells of Badr, approximately 130 km south-west of Madinah
Muslim force313 men, 70 camels, 2 horses, limited weapons
Quraysh force~1,000 men, 700 camels, 100 horses, well-armed
Muslim commanderThe Prophet Muhammad ﷺ
Quraysh commanderAbu Jahl (Amr ibn Hisham)
OutcomeDecisive Muslim victory

Background and causes

After the Hijra (migration) to Madinah in 622 CE, the Quraysh continued to persecute Muslims and seized the property they had left behind in Makkah. The Muslims had authority from Allah to defend themselves (Surah Al-Hajj 22:39).

Immediate cause: The Prophet ﷺ sent a force to intercept a wealthy Quraysh trade caravan returning from Syria, led by Abu Sufyan. Abu Sufyan sent a messenger to Makkah requesting an army, then took an alternate route and escaped. The Quraysh army of 1,000 continued towards Madinah even after learning the caravan was safe — driven by pride and a desire to crush Islam once and for all.

Key events

  1. The Prophet ﷺ consulted his Companions (shura). The Ansar (Madinan helpers) pledged full support, which moved him deeply.
  2. The Muslim army reached Badr first and secured the water wells.
  3. The Prophet ﷺ prayed throughout the night, beseeching Allah's help. Abu Bakr (RA) kept vigil with him.
  4. The battle began with single combat (mubarzah): three champions from each side. The Muslim champions — Ali, Hamza and Ubayda (RA) — defeated the Quraysh champions Utbah, Shaybah and Walid.
  5. The Prophet ﷺ threw a handful of sand towards the enemy, praying, and a sudden wind blinded the Quraysh ranks.
  6. Angels descended to assist the Muslims (Surah Al-Imran 3:123–125).
  7. Abu Jahl — the bitterest enemy of Islam — was killed; 70 Quraysh were killed in total and 70 taken prisoner.

Outcome and aftermath

Casualties:
  • Muslims: 14 martyred (6 Muhajirin, 8 Ansar)
  • Quraysh: 70 killed (including Abu Jahl, Utbah, Shaybah, Walid), 70 taken prisoner
Treatment of prisoners: The Prophet ﷺ instructed they be treated with kindness. Literate prisoners who had no money to pay ransom could earn their freedom by teaching ten Muslim children to read and write — a remarkable example of valuing education.

Significance of Badr

  • First military victory — proved that a small, outnumbered Muslim force could succeed with faith and strategy.
  • Yawm al-Furqan ("Day of Criterion") — Allah called it a day that separated truth from falsehood (Surah Al-Anfal 8:41).
  • Divine support — angels fighting alongside the Muslims strengthened the faith of the believers.
  • Political recognition — Madinah's status as an Islamic state was firmly established; surrounding tribes took notice.
  • Quraysh weakened — the loss of their leading men (Abu Jahl, Utbah) seriously disrupted Qurayshi leadership.
Surah Al-Anfal (8:17)
"And you did not kill them, but it was Allah who killed them. And you threw not when you threw, but it was Allah who threw."
This verse, revealed after Badr, reminded Muslims that their victory was ultimately from Allah, not their own strength.

A common error is confusing the numbers: it was 313 Muslims vs ~1,000 Quraysh, not the other way round. The disparity is central to the significance of the Muslim victory.
