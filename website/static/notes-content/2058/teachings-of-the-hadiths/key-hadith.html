Using Hadith in exam answers

When quoting a Hadith in an essay, give: (1) the text (in English), (2) the narrator (e.g. "narrated by Abu Hurairah"), and (3) the collection (e.g. Bukhari, Muslim). Then explain what it teaches and link it to the question. A Hadith cited without any of these components earns reduced marks.

Intentions and sincerity

"Actions are judged by their intentions."
Narrated by Umar ibn al-Khattab (RA). Recorded in Sahih Bukhari and Sahih Muslim.

Lesson: The value of any deed in Islam depends on the niyyah (intention) behind it. A good deed done to show off earns no reward; a small deed done sincerely for Allah is greatly rewarded. Used in questions about sincerity, worship and Islamic ethics.

Brotherhood and compassion

"None of you truly believes until he loves for his brother what he loves for himself."
Narrated by Anas ibn Malik (RA). Recorded in Sahih Bukhari and Sahih Muslim.

Lesson: True faith (iman) requires wishing good for others. Selfishness and jealousy contradict the spirit of Islam. Used in questions about Islamic brotherhood (Ummah), social conduct and the importance of good character.

"The believer is a mirror for his fellow believer."
Recorded in Abu Dawud and Tirmidhi.

Lesson: Muslims should help one another to recognise their faults gently and with good will. Used in questions about Islamic social conduct and the duty of Muslims towards each other.

Learning and teaching the Quran

"The best of you are those who learn the Quran and teach it to others."
Narrated by Uthman ibn Affan (RA). Recorded in Sahih Bukhari.

Lesson: Learning the Quran and spreading its knowledge is among the most virtuous deeds. Used in questions about the importance of the Quran and Islamic education.

Honesty and truthfulness

"Truthfulness leads to righteousness and righteousness leads to Paradise."
Narrated by Abdullah ibn Masud (RA). Recorded in Sahih Bukhari and Sahih Muslim.

Lesson: Honesty is the foundation of good character in Islam, and it leads ultimately to reward in the Hereafter. Used in questions about Islamic ethics, moral character and truthfulness.

Good character and conduct

"Allah does not look at your faces and wealth, but He looks at your hearts and deeds."
Narrated by Abu Hurairah (RA). Recorded in Sahih Muslim.

Lesson: Allah judges people by their sincerity and actions, not by wealth or outward appearance. Equality before Allah. Used in questions about Islamic social justice, equality and the importance of good deeds.

"The most beloved of deeds to Allah are those done consistently, even if small."
Narrated by Aisha (RA). Recorded in Sahih Bukhari and Sahih Muslim.

Lesson: Consistency in worship and good deeds is more valued than occasional large acts. Emphasises the importance of regular daily practice. Used in questions about worship, Salah and the importance of routine in Islamic life.

Mercy and kindness

"Show mercy to those on earth, and the One in heaven will show mercy to you."
Narrated by Abdullah ibn Amr (RA). Recorded in Tirmidhi.

Lesson: Allah's mercy is conditional on showing mercy to others. Used in questions about Islamic ethics, the Prophet's character and treatment of others.

"There is a reward for serving any living being."
Narrated by Abu Hurairah (RA). Recorded in Sahih Bukhari and Sahih Muslim.

Lesson: Kindness to all creatures — humans and animals — earns a reward from Allah. Used in questions about Islamic ethics, compassion and treatment of animals.

The Five Pillars

"Islam is built on five [pillars]: bearing witness that there is no god but Allah and that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah, establishing the Salah, paying the Zakat, making pilgrimage to the House, and fasting in Ramadan."
Narrated by Ibn Umar (RA). Recorded in Sahih Bukhari and Sahih Muslim.

Lesson: Lists all five pillars explicitly in one Hadith. Useful in introductions to any essay about worship or the pillars of Islam.

When writing an essay introduction, opening with this Hadith on the five pillars and then narrowing to the specific pillar you are discussing is a high-scoring strategy — it shows breadth of knowledge and a structured approach.
Do not confuse the narrator with the collector. Abu Hurairah is a narrator (Companion of the Prophet); Bukhari is a collector who recorded the Hadith in his book. Write "narrated by Abu Hurairah and recorded in Sahih Bukhari" — not "narrated by Bukhari."
How to use a Hadith in an essay paragraph (model answer)

Question: "Explain the importance of sincerity in Islamic worship."

Model paragraph:

Sincerity of intention (niyyah) is central to all acts of worship in Islam. The Prophet ﷺ said, "Actions are judged by their intentions" (narrated by Umar ibn al-Khattab RA, recorded in Bukhari and Muslim). This Hadith teaches that the reward for any deed depends not on its outward appearance but on the intention behind it. A person who performs Salah to be seen by others gains no spiritual reward, whilst a person who performs even a small act of kindness purely for Allah's pleasure is greatly rewarded. This shows that inward sincerity is the foundation of all Islamic worship, not merely outward compliance with rules.

Note the structure: quotation → source → explanation → link to the question.
