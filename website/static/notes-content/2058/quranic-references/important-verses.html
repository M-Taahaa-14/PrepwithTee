How to use Quranic references in exams

The Cambridge examiner's formula: every mark-earning point in an Islamiat essay must have three parts:
  1. Reference — quote the verse (in English) and give the surah name + approximate verse number.
  2. Explanation — explain what it means in plain language.
  3. Relevance — link it directly to the question being asked.
A bare quotation without explanation typically scores 1 mark at most.

Nature of Allah (Tawhid)

Surah Al-Ikhlas (112:1–4)
"Say: He is Allah, the One; Allah, the Eternal Refuge; He neither begets nor is born; Nor is there to Him any equivalent."

Lesson: The absolute oneness (Tawhid) of Allah — He has no partners, no parents, no children. Used in questions about the nature of Allah and the concept of Tawhid.

Ayat al-Kursi — Surah Al-Baqarah (2:255)
"Allah — there is no deity except Him, the Ever-Living, the Sustainer of existence. Neither drowsiness overtakes Him nor sleep."

Lesson: Allah's eternal, self-sustaining nature. He is fully aware of all things. Used for questions on the attributes of Allah.

The Holy Quran

First Revelation — Surah Al-Alaq (96:1)
"Read in the name of your Lord who created."

Lesson: The first word revealed was 'Iqra' (Read/Recite). Emphasises the importance of knowledge in Islam. Used in questions about the first revelation and the importance of learning.

Protection of the Quran — Surah Al-Hijr (15:9)
"Verily, it is We Who have sent down the Reminder, and We will surely preserve it."

Lesson: Allah guarantees the Quran's protection from corruption for all time. Used when discussing why the Quran remains unchanged unlike earlier scriptures.

Guidance for Mankind — Surah Al-Baqarah (2:2)
"This is the Book about which there is no doubt, a guidance for those conscious of Allah."

Lesson: The Quran is described as the definitive guidance. Used in questions about the importance and purpose of the Quran.

The Prophet ﷺ

Mercy to the Worlds — Surah Al-Anbiya (21:107)
"And We have not sent you except as a mercy to the worlds."

Lesson: The Prophet ﷺ was sent as a blessing for all humanity, not just Arabs or Muslims. Used in questions about his role and character.

Best Example — Surah Al-Ahzab (33:21)
"Indeed, in the Messenger of Allah you have an excellent example for whoever has hope in Allah and the Last Day and remembers Allah often."

Lesson: The Prophet ﷺ is the perfect model of conduct (Uswa Hasana). Used in questions about why Muslims follow the Sunnah.

Salah (Prayer)

Obligation of Salah — Surah Al-Baqarah (2:43)
"And establish prayer and give zakat and bow with those who bow [in worship and obedience]."

Lesson: Salah and Zakat are directly commanded by Allah. Used in questions about the importance of daily prayer.

Prescribed Times — Surah An-Nisa (4:103)
"Indeed, prayer has been decreed upon the believers a decree of specified times."

Lesson: Salah must be performed at its appointed times — it is not optional to delay or reschedule it freely. Used to explain why Muslims pray five times at specific times.

Sawm (Fasting)

Command to Fast — Surah Al-Baqarah (2:183)
"O you who have believed, decreed upon you is fasting as it was decreed upon those before you that you may become righteous."

Lesson: Fasting is obligatory and its purpose is taqwa (God-consciousness/righteousness). Used in questions about the significance of Sawm.

Hajj (Pilgrimage)

Obligation of Hajj — Surah Al-Imran (3:97)
"And pilgrimage to the House is a duty owed to Allah by people who are able to undertake it."

Lesson: Hajj is obligatory for Muslims who are physically and financially capable. Used in questions about the conditions and importance of Hajj.

Zakat

Those Who Pay Zakat — Surah Al-Mu'minun (23:4)
"And they who are observant of zakat."

Lesson: Paying zakat is listed as a quality of the true believers. Used when discussing the importance of zakat as a pillar of Islam.

In the exam, you do not need to give an exact verse number — the surah name is sufficient. However, being able to say "approximately verse 183 of Surah Al-Baqarah" shows depth of knowledge and may earn an additional credit mark.
Never translate a verse loosely from memory and present it as a quotation. If you are not sure of the exact wording, write "Allah says in Surah X that…" and paraphrase accurately — this is safer than a misquotation, which examiners deduct marks for.
